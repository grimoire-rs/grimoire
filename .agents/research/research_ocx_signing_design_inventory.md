## Direct Answer

ocx ships keyless (Sigstore Fulcio + Rekor v1) and key-pair signing. A signature is a Sigstore bundle v0.3 holding a DSSE-enveloped in-toto Statement (predicate `https://sigstore.dev/cosign/sign/v1`) over the manifest digest, pushed as an OCI referrer (Referrers API, or the `sha256-<hex>` fallback index on registries without one) and readable by stock `cosign verify`. In ocx, "sidecar" means cosign's legacy tag-only shape: an ordinary mutable tag `sha256-<hex>.sig` / `.att` / `.sbom` beside the subject, outside the referrers listing; ocx reads `.sig` and `.att` sidecars through the same trust gate as bundles (keyless ones need log evidence unless `--allow-unlogged-signature`), lists `.sbom` as always-unsigned, and writes a `.sig` sidecar only when asked (`--signature-format simplesigning|both`). Verification runs at install/pull after the manifest digest resolves and before any layer download, as two ANDed rounds: index-declared `publishers` (carried in the package root document, claims matched by Fulcio extension) then operator `[[trust.policy]]` from `config.toml` tiers; the project `ocx.toml` policy gates only `ocx package verify`, never install. Per-registry `signatures = if_declared | required | off` (default `if_declared`) governs the publisher round; a package neither declared nor policy-covered installs unverified with an INFO line, and `--no-verify` / `OCX_NO_VERIFY` skip with one warning unless a system-tier lock pins `required` (exit 81 `no_verify_refused`). Trust material resolves through a six-rung ladder ending in the public-good root over TUF (`tough` via sigstore-rs), with a 24 h trust-root cache that makes `--offline` verify work. The index-declared publisher list is not itself authenticated until "slice 4" (catalog signature), which is designed and not built; ocx records this as threat T1 "partial". Release signing of ocx's own packages and the `ocx.sh` publisher backfill (slices 2-3) are also designed, not shipped.
